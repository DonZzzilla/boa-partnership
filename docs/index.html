// 01 - THE COMMUNICATION PROBLEM

Where Information Breaks Down

BOA volunteers coach new players through their first sessions in Ghosts of Tabor every day. But volunteer numbers are at an all-time low — 20-30 active, down from a historical average of 45+. The high bar of Discord knowledge needed to run processes drives people away. Volunteers give up their time for a crumb of acknowledgment. Better communication with CWS — even a bi-annual visit to our monthly meetings — would show volunteers they matter.

▶ Manual Processes CRITICAL

Ticket counting, session tracking, scheduling, and recognition are all handled by hand. Information gets delayed or lost because humans are manually moving data around.

▶ Knowledge Walks Out HIGH

When experienced volunteers depart, institutional memory leaves with them. Critical knowledge lives in Discord messages and tribal understanding.

▶ Volunteers Feel Invisible HIGH

Contributions disappear into the void. Limited formal acknowledgment means volunteers don't know their work matters.

▶ Players Seek Answers MEDIUM

Without official information, speculation fills the gap. Community members reverse-engineer answers that could be communicated directly.

▶ Volunteers Give, Get Little Back HIGH

Volunteers give up their time for a crumb of acknowledgment. No direct line to CWS means they don't know if their work matters. A bi-annual visit to BOA meetings would change that.

// 02 - THE SEVEN ASKS

Seven Focused Asks

Each ask addresses a specific communication, retention, or information flow problem.

▶ Volunteer Burnout CRITICAL

41% of departing volunteers cite burnout. Repetitive manual tasks consume time that skilled volunteers could spend on high-impact work.

▶ Knowledge Retention HIGH

When experienced volunteers depart, institutional memory walks out the door. Only 25% of decisions are documented.

▶ Operational Burden MEDIUM

35% of volunteer hours are spent on repetitive, automatable tasks. Monthly reporting takes ~20 person-hours.

▶ EU Coverage Gap MEDIUM

Leadership departures created responsiveness gaps during EU prime-time hours.

// 04 - THE UNIFYING THEME

What Ties It All Together

If asked: what's the common thread between all seven requests?

The Common Thread

Every request reduces friction between people. Whether it's volunteers communicating with leadership, BOAs communicating with players, or developers communicating with the community.

● Information Flow

Better data sharing means faster, more accurate communication across the entire volunteer ecosystem.

● Retention

When volunteers feel heard and recognized, they stay. Recognition is communication.

● Knowledge Preservation

Capturing institutional knowledge means communication survives leadership transitions.

● Developer-Community Bridge

Official information reduces speculation and builds trust between CWS and the player base.

Our approach: We're not asking for compensation or proprietary access. We're asking for better tools to communicate with each other.
