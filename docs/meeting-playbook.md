## Purpose of This Document

This playbook is your tactical guide for the CWS partnership meeting. It covers what to say, what not to say, how to handle pushback, and how to steer the conversation toward a "yes." Read it in full before the meeting. Bring it with you as a reference.

## Meeting Objectives (Ranked by Priority)

1. **Secure agreement in principle** to a phased partnership framework
2. **Identify 2–3 quick-win initiatives** CWS can commit to within 30 days

4. **Surface CWS's internal priorities and constraints** so we can tailor the next proposal

## Opening Script (First 9 Minutes)

The opening sets the tone. Do not start with slides. Start with a story.

### Minute 1–3: The Hook

> "Before we get into the slides, I want to share something that happened last week. One of our volunteers — someone who joined BOA three months ago as a complete newcomer — independently rebuilt a critical section of our onboarding wiki after a data loss incident. She spent an entire weekend on it. She didn't ask for credit. She just saw a problem and fixed it. That's the kind of people BOA attracts. And it's exactly why we're here today — because people like her deserve better infrastructure, better recognition, and better support than we can provide alone."

**Why this works:** It leads with a human story, not a slide. It demonstrates BOA's value through action, not claims. It creates an emotional anchor that the rest of the presentation builds on.

### Minute 3–6: The Bridge

> "BOA has been the operational backbone of the Ghost of Tabor community for years. We've coordinated 150+ events, mentored 50+ contributors into leadership roles, and maintained a suite of tools the entire community depends on. But we've reached an inflection point. Our volunteer retention has dropped from 48% to 35% at the 12-month mark. Our best people are spending 35% of their time on tasks that could be automated. And our institutional knowledge is concentrated in a handful of individuals — if they step away, we lose years of accumulated expertise."

**Why this works:** It establishes credibility, then introduces urgency with specific numbers. It frames the problem as a natural consequence of growth, not a failure.

### Minute 6–9: The Ask

> "We're not here to ask for a blank check. We're here to propose a partnership — a phased, measurable collaboration that starts with quick wins and builds toward transformative impact. We've identified four initiative areas, and today I'd like to walk you through each one, hear your reactions, and together figure out where to start. Our goal for this meeting is simple: agree on a date within the next two weeks for a working session where we map out a 90-day roadmap together."

**Why this works:** It's specific, low-risk, and collaborative. The ask is a working session, not a commitment. It positions BOA as a partner, not a supplicant.

## Discussion Topics Ranked by Likelihood of Approval

Use this ranking to manage the conversation. Lead with high-probability items to build momentum. Save lower-probability items for later, after you've established agreement on the easier wins.

| Rank | Topic | Likelihood | Why | Strategic Value |
|------|-------|-----------|-----|-----------------|
| 1 | **Volunteer Recognition Program** | **HIGH** | Low cost, high emotional impact, easy to pilot, co-branding opportunity for CWS | Builds goodwill, creates visible proof of partnership |
| 2 | **Knowledge Base Initiative** | **HIGH** | Addresses a clear pain point (knowledge loss), uses existing tools, no licensing costs | Foundational — makes every other initiative more effective |
| 3 | **Communication Improvements** | **MEDIUM-HIGH** | CWS already knows this is a problem; proposals are practical and non-discretionary | Demonstrates BOA's operational maturity |
| 4 | **EU Coverage Expansion** | **MEDIUM** | Logical need, but requires recruitment commitment from BOA side | Shows BOA is thinking about community-wide impact |
| 5 | **Knowledge Retention Protocols** | **MEDIUM** | Important but abstract; harder to quantify immediate value | Critical for long-term sustainability |

## Likely Objections from CWS — and Recommended Responses

### Objection 1: "This seems like a lot. Where do we even start?"

**What they're really saying:** We're interested but overwhelmed. Give us a clear on-ramp.

**Response:**
> "You're right — it is a lot if you look at it all at once. That's exactly why we're proposing a phased approach. We're not asking you to approve everything today. We're asking you to agree to a working session where we prioritize together. The first phase is deliberately small: launch the recognition program and start building the knowledge base. Both are low-cost, high-impact, and we can have them running within 30 days. Everything else builds on that foundation — but only if and when you're ready."

**Tactical note:** Have a one-page "Phase 1 Only" summary ready to share if this objection comes up. It should fit on a single page with 5–7 bullet points.

---

### Objection 2: "We don't have budget for a bot development project."

**What they're really saying:** We're worried about cost. We need to understand the investment.

**Response:**
> "Totally understood — and we want to be upfront about costs. The BOA Bot is a medium-term initiative, not a Day 1 ask. In Phase 1, we're only asking for collaborative scoping — no development budget needed yet. When we do get to development, we've designed the bot to run on infrastructure that costs approximately $40/month. The time savings alone — we estimate 640–876 volunteer hours per year — represent a return of roughly $16,000–$22,000 annually against a $480 annual hosting cost. But let's not get ahead of ourselves. First, let's see if the partnership makes sense at all."

**Tactical note:** Never lead with cost. Lead with value. Mention cost only when asked, and always frame it against the return.

---

### Objection 3: "Why should CWS invest in BOA's internal operations?"

**What they're really saying:** What's in it for us?

**Response:**
> "Great question, and one we want to answer directly. BOA's operational health directly impacts the entire Ghost of Tabor community — which is CWS's community too. When BOA volunteers burn out or disengage, the community loses mentorship, event coordination, and support infrastructure. When knowledge is lost because someone leaves without documentation, the whole ecosystem suffers. Investing in BOA's operations is investing in the community's foundation. And specifically for CWS, this partnership offers co-branding visibility, community goodwill, and a model for volunteer management that other guilds and communities will look to as a best practice. This isn't charity — it's strategic community investment."

**Tactical note:** Always tie BOA's health back to CWS's interests. Never frame it as "help us" — frame it as "invest in the community ecosystem."

---

### Objection 4: "We've tried recognition programs before. They fizzle out."

**What they're really saying:** We're skeptical about sustainability. Show us this is different.

**Response:**
> "That's a fair concern, and it's one we've designed specifically to address. The difference between this program and a typical 'employee of the month' setup is threefold. First, it's integrated into the tools volunteers already use — the recognition flows through Discord, not a separate platform they have to check. Second, it's automated where possible — milestone detection, hour tracking, and notification triggers run without manual effort. Third, it's tied to tangible career outcomes — recommendation letters, service records, and professional development resources. This isn't a plaque on a wall. It's a system that helps volunteers build real-world value from their community service. And because it's phased, we can start small, prove it works, and scale it — rather than overcommitting and underdelivering."

---

### Objection 5: "How do we know BOA's leadership can execute on this?"

**What they're really saying:** We need confidence in your organizational capacity.

**Response:**
> "I appreciate the directness. Here's what I'd point to: BOA has been operating and delivering results for years without any formal partnership or external support. We've coordinated 150+ events, maintained 12 open-source tools, and built a mentorship pipeline that has advanced 50+ contributors into leadership roles. What we're proposing isn't a transformation of what BOA does — it's an investment in doing it better, more sustainably, and at greater scale. And the phased approach means you'll see results from Phase 1 before we ask for commitment to Phase 2. We're putting our credibility on the line with every phase."

**Tactical note:** If you have specific metrics or testimonials from community members, reference them here. Concrete evidence beats general claims.

---

### Objection 6: "We need to discuss this internally. We can't make a decision today."

**What they're really saying:** This is bigger than the people in the room, or they need to manage internal politics.

**Response:**
> "Absolutely — we'd be concerned if you made a decision this significant on the spot. What we'd suggest is this: we'll send you a one-page summary of what we discussed today, including the proposed Phase 1 initiatives and the suggested working session format. If you could identify the right stakeholders for that working session and give us a date within the next two weeks, that would be ideal. We're also happy to do a follow-up call with any team members who weren't in this meeting but need to be part of the decision."

**Tactical note:** Always have a "next step" ready when you hear this objection. The goal is to convert "we need to think about it" into a concrete calendar commitment.

---

### Objection 7: "The bot sounds interesting, but we're concerned about security and data."

**What they're really saying:** We've been burned before, or we have compliance obligations.

**Response:**
> "Security is something we take extremely seriously, and it's built into the bot's design from the ground up. The bot uses role-based access control — it only accesses data that aligns with each user's role. All data is encrypted at rest and in transit. We follow data minimization principles — we only collect what's necessary. CWS retains full data ownership at all times. And the bot is designed with GDPR principles in mind, including right to deletion and data portability. We've included a full security review in the proposal document, and we're happy to walk through it in detail — or have your technical team review it independently. We welcome that scrutiny."

**Tactical note:** Don't be defensive about security concerns. Welcome them. It shows maturity and builds trust.

---

### Objection 8: "What happens if the partnership doesn't work out?"

**What they're really saying:** We need an exit ramp.

**Response:**
> "That's a smart question, and we think about it too. That's why the partnership is designed with clear phase gates. At the end of each phase, we jointly assess: Is this delivering value? Should we continue, adjust, or stop? There's no long-term lock-in. And because we've designed the knowledge base and recognition systems to be owned and operated by BOA — with CWS as a partner, not a vendor — there's no dependency risk. If the partnership ends, BOA keeps everything we've built together. You're not locked into a platform or a contract."

## Questions to AVOID

These questions derail the conversation, put you on the defensive, or open doors you don't want to walk through.

### 1. "What's your budget for this?"
**Why to avoid:** You're asking them to commit to a number before they've committed to the concept. It shifts the conversation from value to cost.

**What to say instead:** "We've designed this to be cost-effective and phased. Let's first agree on the initiatives that make the most sense, and then we'll provide a detailed cost breakdown for each phase."

### 2. "Can you build [unscoped feature]?"
**Why to avoid:** Feature requests without scope lead to scope creep and set unrealistic expectations.

**What to say instead:** "That's an interesting idea. Let's capture it in our working session notes and evaluate it as part of the roadmap prioritization."

### 3. "Why hasn't BOA fixed these problems already?"
**Why to avoid:** This puts you on the defensive and invites a post-mortem instead of a forward-looking conversation.

**What to say instead:** "We've made significant progress with limited resources — the 150+ events and 50+ mentees are proof of that. But there's a difference between what a volunteer team can do alone and what's possible with strategic partnership. That's why we're here."

### 4. "What do other partners get?" / "Who else is doing this?"
**Why to avoid:** This invites comparison and commoditizes the partnership.

**What to say instead:** "Every partnership is tailored to the specific community's needs. What we've proposed here is designed specifically for BOA and CWS based on our understanding of your community's challenges and opportunities."

### 5. "Can we see the bot working today?"
**Why to avoid:** The bot doesn't exist yet. Showing nothing is worse than showing a prototype.

**What to say instead:** "The bot is in the proposal stage — we wanted to validate the concept and secure partnership alignment before investing in development. If we agree to move forward, we'd target an MVP within 8–12 weeks. In the meantime, we can show you the workflow designs and the analytics dashboard mockups."

### 6. "What about [unrelated community issue]?"
**Why to avoid:** Scope creep. The meeting is about the partnership proposal, not every community challenge.

**What to say instead:** "That's an important topic, and I want to give it the attention it deserves. Can we schedule a separate conversation to focus on that specifically? Today, I want to make sure we do justice to the partnership proposal."

## Questions to PRIORITIZE

These questions advance the conversation, surface valuable information, and build alignment.

### 1. "What are CWS's top community priorities for the next 12 months?"
**Why ask:** This tells you where BOA's initiatives align with CWS's strategic goals. It lets you customize your pitch in real time.

**How to use the answer:** If they say "retention," emphasize the Recognition Program. If they say "community growth," emphasize EU coverage and the Knowledge Base. If they say "operational efficiency," emphasize the BOA Bot.

### 2. "What does success look like for you in a partnership like this?"
**Why ask:** This reveals their success criteria and lets you frame your proposals in their language.

**How to use the answer:** Echo their success criteria back when presenting initiatives. "You mentioned that success means measurable improvement in volunteer retention — our Recognition Program is specifically designed to address that, with a target of 30–40% improvement."

### 3. "Are there any initiatives already underway that we should be aware of?"
**Why ask:** Avoids duplication, identifies potential synergies, and shows you're collaborative, not competitive.

**How to use the answer:** If they mention existing efforts, position BOA's proposals as complementary. "That's great — our Knowledge Base Initiative could serve as the documentation backbone for that work."

### 4. "Who else needs to be involved in the decision-making process?"
**Why ask:** Identifies stakeholders, influencers, and potential blockers. Helps you plan your follow-up strategy.

**How to use the answer:** If they mention specific people, ask if those individuals can be included in the working session. If they can't, ask for a briefing you can provide to them.

### 5. "What's the biggest risk you see in this partnership?"
**Why ask:** Surfaces unspoken concerns and gives you a chance to address them proactively.

**How to use the answer:** Take the concern seriously. Don't dismiss it. Offer a specific mitigation. "That's a valid concern. Here's how we've addressed it in our design..."

### 6. "If you were in our position, what would you prioritize first?"
**Why ask:** This invites CWS to co-own the prioritization. People support what they help create.

**How to use the answer:** If their priority aligns with your Phase 1, great — confirm and reinforce. If it doesn't, acknowledge their input and explain your sequencing logic. "That's a great candidate for Phase 2. Here's why we recommend starting with [X] first..."

### 7. "What would make this partnership a 'no-brainer' for you?"
**Why ask:** This is the closing question. It forces a direct answer about what they need to say yes.

**How to use the answer:** Whatever they say, write it down. That's your closing argument. "Based on what you've just said, it sounds like [X] is the key factor. Let me show you exactly how our proposal delivers on that..."

## Negotiation Strategy

### Guiding Principles

1. **Never negotiate against yourself.** Don't offer concessions before CWS asks for them. Present the proposal as designed, and let them tell you what they want to change.

2. **Trade, don't concede.** If CWS wants to remove something, ask for something in return. "We can defer the BOA Bot to Phase 3 if you'd like — in exchange, could we get CWS's commitment to co-brand the Recognition Program materials?"

3. **Anchor high, phase generously.** Present the full vision (all four initiative areas). When CWS pushes back on scope, "compromise" by agreeing to phase the work — which was your plan all along.

4. **Use the "working session" as your closer.** The primary ask is not "approve the partnership." It's "schedule a working session." This is a low-commitment, high-value next step that keeps the conversation alive.

5. **Let silence work.** After making a key point or asking a key question. Don't rush to fill the silence. Let CWS process and respond.

### Concession Hierarchy (What to Give Up If Needed)

If CWS pushes back on scope, use this hierarchy to make concessions without sacrificing core value:

| Priority | Initiative | Can Defer? | Concession Cost |
|----------|-----------|------------|-----------------|
| **Protect** | Recognition Program (Phase 1) | No | Core quick win — non-negotiable |
| **Protect** | Knowledge Base (Phase 1 MVP) | No | Core quick win — non-negotiable |
| **Flexible** | BOA Bot timeline | Yes — defer to Phase 3 | Low — it's already planned as medium-term |
| **Flexible** | EU Coverage expansion | Yes — defer to Phase 3 | Medium — important but not urgent |
| **Flexible** | Co-branding scope | Yes — start minimal, expand later | Low — easy to add later |
| **Flexible** | Technical knowledge sharing | Yes — defer to Phase 3 | Low — requires partnership maturity first |
| **Trade** | Impact Awards frequency | Yes — quarterly → semi-annually | Low — still valuable at reduced frequency |
| **Trade** | Recommendation letter author pool | Yes — start with 2–3 authors | Low — expand as program matures |

### What NOT to Concede

- **The phased approach itself.** This is your risk management strategy. Never agree to an all-at-once implementation.
- **Data ownership.** CWS must retain full ownership of all community data. This is non-negotiable.
- **BOA's operational autonomy.** The partnership enhances BOA's operations; it doesn't transfer control to CWS.
- **The working session.** If they won't commit to a working session, you don't have a partnership — you have a polite "maybe." The working session is the minimum viable next step.

## Cross-Cutting Topics: Tactical Notes

These three topics are important but should not dominate the conversation. Address them briefly and tie them back to the core initiatives.

### EU Coverage

**Key message:** "A significant portion of Ghost of Tabor's player base is in EU time zones. Right now, BOA's coverage during EU prime-time hours has gaps due to volunteer turnover. We want to recruit and empower EU-based volunteers — and the Knowledge Base and Recognition Program will make that recruitment and onboarding dramatically more effective."

**Tie to core initiatives:** EU coverage is a recruitment and onboarding problem. The Knowledge Base and Recognition Program are the solutions. This doesn't need a separate initiative — it's a benefit of the core initiatives.

**If asked about timeline:** "We'd target EU recruitment as a Phase 2 initiative, once the Knowledge Base is live and the Recognition Program is established. That gives us the infrastructure to onboard and retain EU volunteers effectively."

### Knowledge Retention

**Key message:** "BOA's institutional knowledge is one of its greatest assets — and its greatest vulnerability. Right now, critical operational knowledge is concentrated in a small number of long-standing members. The Knowledge Base Initiative is specifically designed to address this, with departure protocols, decision logs, and living documentation practices."

**Tie to core initiatives:** Knowledge retention IS the Knowledge Base Initiative. Don't let it become a separate conversation.

**If asked about current state:** Be honest. "We've already experienced situations where departing volunteers took critical knowledge with them. We've been rebuilding from scratch. That's not sustainable, and it's the primary driver behind the Knowledge Base proposal."

### Leadership Gaps

**Key message:** "BOA has experienced leadership transitions recently — which is natural for a volunteer organization. But it's highlighted the need for structured succession planning and leadership development. We see this as a Phase 2–3 initiative, built on the foundation of the Knowledge Base (which preserves leadership knowledge) and the Recognition Program (which identifies and develops emerging leaders)."

**Tie to core initiatives:** Leadership gaps are addressed by the Knowledge Base (knowledge preservation) and the Recognition Program (leadership pipeline development). Frame it as a natural outcome of the core initiatives, not a separate problem.

**If pressed on specifics:** "We'd like to work with CWS to design a leadership development track — perhaps including mentorship pairings, shadowing opportunities, and joint workshops. But that's a conversation for the working session, once we've aligned on the partnership framework."

## Closing Strategy

### The Ideal Close

> "Thank you all for your time and your thoughtful questions. Here's what I'm hearing: [summarize 2–3 key themes from the discussion]. Based on that, I think our next step is clear. We'd like to propose a working session — maybe 90 minutes — where we bring together the right stakeholders from both sides, prioritize the Phase 1 initiatives together, and map out a 90-day roadmap. Would [two weeks from now] work for that? We'll send a calendar invite with an agenda and a pre-read document."

### The "Hesitant" Close

If CWS is positive but non-committal:

> "I understand this is a significant decision, and you'll want to discuss it internally. What we'll do is send you a one-page summary of today's conversation, including the Phase 1 proposals and the suggested working session format. Could we tentatively hold [date two weeks out] for the working session? If the timing doesn't work, we can adjust — but having a date on the calendar keeps the momentum going."

### The "Pushback" Close

If CWS raises significant concerns:

> "I appreciate the candor, and these are exactly the kinds of concerns we should surface early. What I'd suggest is this: let's capture these concerns in writing, and we'll prepare detailed responses — including adjustments to the proposal if needed — for the working session. That way, we're not trying to resolve everything in this meeting. We'll come prepared to address each concern specifically. Can we schedule that working session for [date]?"

### What to Send After the Meeting

Within 24 hours of the meeting, send:

1. **Thank-you message** (personal, referencing specific moments from the conversation)
2. **One-page meeting summary** (key discussion points, agreed next steps, working session date)
3. **Working session pre-read** (agenda, attendee list, Phase 1 proposal summary)
4. **Full proposal package** (link to all documents, organized by initiative area)

## Risk Scenarios and Contingencies

### Scenario 1: CWS Wants to Start with the Bot (Not the Quick Wins)

**Risk:** The Bot is the most complex initiative. Starting there without the foundation of Recognition + Knowledge Base increases failure risk.

**Response:** "The Bot is absolutely a centerpiece of the partnership — and we're excited about it. Our recommendation would be to do a 4-week discovery and scoping phase for the Bot (which requires minimal commitment) while simultaneously launching the Recognition Program and Knowledge Base. That way, when Bot development starts, it's building on a foundation of documented processes and engaged volunteers. Would that phased approach work for you?"

### Scenario 2: CWS Wants to Strip Down to One Initiative

**Risk:** A single initiative doesn't demonstrate the partnership's full value.

**Response:** "We can absolutely start with one initiative. Our recommendation would be the Recognition Program, since it's the lowest-cost, highest-visibility quick win. But we'd suggest framing it as 'Phase 1 of the partnership' rather than 'the partnership' — leaving the door open for the Knowledge Base and Bot as the relationship proves out. Can we agree to that framing?"

### Scenario 3: CWS Brings Up a Competing Priority

**Risk:** The conversation shifts to CWS's agenda, not the partnership proposal.

**Response:** "That's a really important priority, and I can see how it connects to what we're proposing. Let's capture it and make sure it's on the agenda for the working session, where we can explore how BOA's initiatives might support that goal. For today, I want to make sure we give the partnership proposal the focus it deserves — and then we can broaden the conversation in the working session."

### Scenario 4: Key Decision-Maker Is Absent

**Risk:** The people in the room can't commit, and the meeting becomes informational rather than decisive.

**Response:** "We want to make sure we're having this conversation with the right people in the room. Would it make sense to reschedule for a time when [key decision-maker] can join? In the meantime, we'll send over the proposal package so everyone can come prepared."

## Tone and Demeanor Guidelines

- **Be confident, not defensive.** You're proposing a partnership, not asking for a favor.
- **Be specific, not vague.** Use numbers, names, and concrete examples.
- **Be collaborative, not prescriptive.** Use "we" and "together" more than "we need" and "you should."
- **Be honest about challenges.** CWS will respect transparency more than spin.
- **Be concise.** If a question can be answered in 30 seconds, don't take 2 minutes.
- **Be human.** This is a community conversation, not a boardroom pitch. Let your genuine care for BOA's volunteers show.
