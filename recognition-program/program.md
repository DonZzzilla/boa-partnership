## 1. Program Overview

### 1.1 Mission Statement

The BOA Volunteer Recognition Program exists to ensure that every individual who contributes their time, energy, and expertise to the Battlefield Operation and Awareness feels genuinely seen, valued, and supported — not just within our community, but in their professional lives beyond it.

### 1.2 The Problem We're Solving

BOA's volunteers are extraordinary. They mentor new players, coordinate events, maintain wikis, build tools, manage communications, and hold the community together through incidents and transitions. Yet today, recognition for these contributions is largely informal — a thank-you in chat, a passing mention in a meeting, a reaction on a message. Those gestures matter, but they are inconsistent, they don't scale, and they leave no lasting record.

The data is clear: **33% of departing volunteers cite lack of recognition as a primary reason for leaving**, and **78% of current volunteers say more recognition would significantly increase their satisfaction**. Recognition is not a luxury — it is a retention strategy, a culture-builder, and a statement of organizational values.

### 1.3 Our Vision

We propose a structured, multi-layered Recognition Program that treats volunteer service as the professional-grade contribution it truly is. The program encompasses four pillars:

1. **Career Value** — Framing BOA service in terms that translate to professional growth, skill development, and résumé-worthy experience.
2. **Service Records** — Maintaining a persistent, verifiable record of each volunteer's contributions, hours, roles, and impact.
3. **Milestone Recognition** — Celebrating contributors at meaningful intervals with digital badges, certificates, and public acknowledgment.
4. **Professional Recommendation Letters** — Offering personalized, written recommendations that volunteers can use in job applications, academic admissions, and professional networking.

These pillars work together to communicate a single message: *Your work here matters, and we're invested in your success — both inside and outside this community.*

### 1.4 Design Principles

- **Dignity First** — Every element of this program is designed to honor the volunteer's contribution with the same seriousness as professional employment.
- **Inclusive by Default** — Recognition is available to all volunteers, not just those in visible or senior roles. Behind-the-scenes contributions count.
- **Voluntary Participation** — Volunteers opt in to public recognition and recommendation letters. Private service records are maintained by default with volunteer consent.
- **Sustainable & Scalable** — The program is designed to grow with BOA, not to depend on any single person's bandwidth.
- **Mutually Beneficial** — BOA's volunteers gain tangible career assets; BOA and its partners gain a reputation as a community that genuinely invests in its people.

## 2. Volunteer Benefits

### 2.1 Career Value

One of the most powerful ways to retain talented volunteers is to help them see how their community service translates into professional growth. BOA volunteers develop real, marketable skills — but without intentional framing, they may not recognize or articulate that value.

#### 2.1.1 Skills Translation Framework

The Recognition Program includes a **Skills Translation Guide** that maps common BOA volunteer activities to professional competencies:

| BOA Activity | Professional Skills Developed |
|---|---|
| Event coordination & logistics management | Project management, stakeholder communication, budgeting, risk assessment |
| New player mentorship & onboarding | Training & development, coaching, curriculum design, empathy-driven communication |
| Wiki writing & documentation | Technical writing, knowledge management, information architecture |
| Bot development & tooling | Software engineering, API integration, DevOps, testing |
| Community moderation | Conflict resolution, policy enforcement, de-escalation, crisis management |
| Data tracking & analytics | Data collection, reporting, trend analysis, dashboard design |
| Leadership & officer roles | People management, strategic planning, decision-making, delegation |

This guide is provided to all volunteers during onboarding and referenced when generating service records and recommendation letters. It helps volunteers articulate their BOA experience in professional contexts — whether on a résumé, in a job interview, or on a LinkedIn profile.

#### 2.1.2 Professional Development Integration

The program encourages volunteers to set **Personal Development Goals** linked to their BOA service. For example:

- A volunteer interested in project management might take the lead on a multi-phase event series, documenting their planning process and outcomes.
- A volunteer exploring technical writing might own a knowledge-baseDocumentation project from outline to publication.
- A volunteer developing leadership skills might mentor newer volunteers and track their mentees' progress.

These goal-linked contributions are noted in service records and become powerful material for recommendation letters.

#### 2.1.3 Résumé & LinkedIn Guidance

The program provides volunteers with:

- **Résumé bullet point templates** for describing BOA service in professional language.
- **LinkedIn integration suggestions** — how to list BOA involvement under volunteer experience, skills, and endorsements.
- **Portfolio links** — volunteers who create tangible outputs (documentation, code, event plans) are encouraged to link to these in professional profiles.

### 2.2 Service Records

Every BOA volunteer deserves a clear, accurate, and portable record of their contribution. The Recognition Program establishes a formal **Service Record** system that tracks each volunteer's journey.

#### 2.2.1 What's Tracked

Each volunteer's Service Record includes:

- **Volunteer start date** and cumulative tenure
- **Total hours contributed** (auto-tracked where possible, with manual entry for offline contributions)
- **Roles held** — complete history of positions, teams, and responsibilities, with dates
- **Sessions & events participated in** — with dates and role (attendee, organizer, host)
- **Milestones reached** — hours-based (10h, 25h, 50h, 100h, 250h, 500h) and impact-based (first mentee, first event led, documentation published, etc.)
- **Recognitions received** — peer nominations, Impact Awards, milestone badges
- **Skills developed** — self-assessed and peer-endorsed, updated periodically
- **Recommendation letters issued** — date, author, and context

#### 2.2.2 Access & Privacy

- Volunteers can view their own Service Record at any time via a simple bot command (`/my-record`) or web dashboard.
- Service Records are **private by default** — not visible to other community members.
- Volunteers may choose to share specific elements (e.g., milestone badges, total hours) publicly.
- Service Records can be exported as a PDF summary for personal use or shared with the author of a recommendation letter (with volunteer consent).
- Volunteer data is retained per the data retention policy outlined in the BOA Bot Security Review.

#### 2.2.3 Data Integrity

- Automated tracking (via BOA Bot's session attendance and participation logging) reduces manual data entry and improves accuracy.
- Volunteers can flag discrepancies or request additions (e.g., hours for work done outside tracked systems).
- Officers and team leads can verify and co-sign records during role transitions or milestone reviews.

### 2.3 Milestone Recognition

Milestone Recognition celebrates volunteers at meaningful intervals throughout their journey. The goal is to create a steady rhythm of acknowledgment — not just at the end of a long tenure, but at every stage where effort and commitment deserve to be seen.

#### 2.3.1 Tiered Milestone System

The program defines six milestone tiers:

| Tier | Requirement | Recognition |
|---|---|---|
| **Newcomer** | First volunteer action completed | Welcome message with program introduction; digital "BOA Contributor" badge |
| **Dedicated** | 10 hours of service | Digital certificate; public shout-out in announcements channel |
| **Committed** | 50 hours of service | Personalized milestone message from leadership; "Dedicated Servant" badge; name added to community Hall of Service |
| **Pillar** | 100 hours of service | Formal certificate of service (PDF, suitable for framing or professional use); "Pillar of the Community" badge; featured in monthly community spotlight |
| **Cornerstone** | 250 hours of service | Handwritten (or hand-signed digital) note from BOA leadership; "Cornerstone" badge; eligibility for recommendation letter if not yet issued |
| **Legend** | 500 hours of service | Permanent recognition on BOA's Hall of Fame page; "Legend" badge; framed certificate mailed or presented in-person; automatic recommendation letter eligibility with priority drafting |

*In addition to hour-based milestones, the program tracks impact-based milestones: first event organized, first mentee onboarded, first documentation contribution, first incident resolved, leadership role assumed, etc. These are celebrated individually alongside tier milestones.*

#### 2.3.2 Quarterly Impact Awards

Beyond automated milestones, the program features **quarterly peer-nominated Impact Awards** in the following categories:

- **Mentor of the Quarter** — For outstanding guidance and support of new or developing community members.
- **Builder of the Quarter** — For exceptional contributions to tools, systems, documentation, or infrastructure.
- **Community Spirit Award** — For someone who consistently lifts the community's culture, morale, and sense of belonging.
- **Above & Beyond Award** — For a volunteer who stepped up during a critical moment or took on responsibilities far beyond their role.
- **Rising Star** — For a newer volunteer (under 6 months) who has made an outsized impact.

Each award includes:
- A digital badge added to the recipient's profile
- A public announcement in the community with a personalized write-up of their contribution
- A certificate suitable for professional use
- Nomination by peers; selection by a rotating panel of officers and veteran volunteers

#### 2.3.3 Recognition Calendar

Milestone celebrations and Impact Awards follow a predictable cadence:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Ongoing | Automated milestone detection and congratulatory messages |
| Monthly | "Community Spotlight" — one volunteer featured with a brief profile and story |
| Quarterly | Impact Awards ceremony (virtual or during community Gatherings) |
| Annually | Year-in-review impact report; annual recognition event |

### 2.4 Professional Recommendation Letters

The recommendation letter is the cornerstone of the Recognition Program's career-value proposition. For many volunteers — especially students, career changers, and early-career professionals — a well-written recommendation from an experienced community leader can be a significant differentiator.

#### 2.4.1 Why Recommendation Letters Matter

Research consistently shows that volunteer experience is valued by employers, but only when it can be articulated and verified. A professional recommendation letter:

- **Validates the volunteer's contributions** with a credible, named source.
- **Translates community work into professional language** that hiring managers and admissions committees understand.
- **Provides specific examples** of the volunteer's skills, impact, and character — far more powerful than a generic endorsement.
- **Strengthens the volunteer's professional network** by establishing a connection with someone willing to vouch for them.
- **Signals organizational maturity** — a community that takes recognition seriously is a community that takes its people seriously.

For BOA, offering recommendation letters transforms volunteer service from a line on a résumé into a **verifiable professional asset**. It tells every volunteer: *We will go to bat for you.*

#### 2.4.2 Eligibility

Under this program, volunteers become eligible for a written recommendation when they meet **any** of the following criteria:

- **Service milestone:** 100+ hours of verified volunteer service
- **Tenure milestone:** 6+ months of active participation
- **Role milestone:** Served in a leadership or specialized role (officer, team lead, mentor, developer, documentation lead) for 3+ months
- **Referral by leadership:** Any volunteer nominated by a BOA officer or team lead, regardless of hours or tenure, who they believe would benefit from a recommendation

Volunteers may request a letter at any point after meeting eligibility criteria. Letters are not automatic — the volunteer initiates the request (ensuring consent and relevance), and a qualified author is matched to their experience.

#### 2.4.3 Letter Authors

Recommendation letters carry weight proportional to the author's credibility and direct knowledge of the volunteer. The program establishes clear authorship guidelines:

- **Letter authors must have directly observed the volunteer's work** — ideally as their team lead, project lead, or mentor.
- **Authors hold a role of officer or above** in the BOA leadership structure, or are designated Senior Volunteers with recognized standing.
- **If no direct lead is available**, a letter may be co-authored by two peers who can attest to different aspects of the volunteer's contribution.
- **Authors are encouraged to draw from the volunteer's Service Record** for specific data points (hours, roles, milestones) to ground the letter in verifiable facts.

#### 2.4.4 Letter Content & Quality Standards

Every recommendation letter produced under this program follows a consistent structure and meets the following quality standards:

- **Personalized content** — No form letters. Each letter includes specific projects, contributions, and observed qualities unique to the volunteer.
- **Professional framing** — The letter connects the volunteer's BOA service to transferable professional skills and competencies.
- **Warm, sincere tone** — The letter should read as a genuine human endorsement, not a bureaucratic formality.
- **Appropriate length** — Typically one page (300–500 words), sufficient to be substantive without being burdensome to read.
- **Contactable author** — The letter includes the author's name, role, and a note that they can be contacted for verification (with the author's consent).
- **BOA branding** — Letters are formatted with BOA letterhead (digital template) and, where applicable, partner co-branding.

A full letter template is provided in the companion document: `example-letter-template.md`.

#### 2.4.5 Request & Delivery Process

```
1. Volunteer checks eligibility via /rec-letter-status
2. Volunteer submits request via /rec-letter-request
   → Specifies: purpose (job application, academic, general), 
     preferred author (optional), key contributions to highlight
3. System matches with available author
4. Author drafts letter (using template + Service Record data)
5. Letter reviewed by program coordinator for quality & branding
6. Letter delivered to volunteer as PDF
7. Volunteer may request one revision within 14 days
8. Letter logged in volunteer's Service Record
```

**Target turnaround time:** 10 business days from request to delivery.

## 3. Suggested Policy Language

The following policy language is recommended for adoption by BOA leadership to formalize the Recognition Program. This language is designed to be incorporated into BOA's existing governance documents, volunteer agreements, or operational policies.

### 3.1 Recognition Program Policy

> **BOA Volunteer Recognition Program Policy**
>
> The Battlefield Operation and Awareness is committed to recognizing and celebrating the contributions of every volunteer who serves our community. To that end, BOA maintains a formal Volunteer Recognition Program that provides:
>
> a) **Service Records** — A persistent, accurate record of each volunteer's contributions, roles, hours, and milestones, maintained with the volunteer's consent and accessible to them at any time.
>
> b) **Milestone Recognition** — Structured acknowledgment of volunteer contributions at defined intervals, including digital badges, certificates, public celebration, and community spotlights.
>
> c) **Professional Recommendation Letters** — Personalized, written recommendations for eligible volunteers, authored by qualified members of the BOA leadership team, suitable for use in professional and academic contexts.
>
> d) **Career Value Support** — Resources and guidance to help volunteers translate their BOA service into professional development, including skills mapping, résumé guidance, and portfolio-building support.
>
> All active volunteers are enrolled in the Recognition Program by default. Participation in public recognition elements (spotlights, awards, public milestone announcements) is voluntary and may be opted in or out at any time via the BOA Bot or by contacting a program coordinator.
>
> The Recognition Program is administered by the BOA Volunteer Program Coordinator (or equivalent role) in partnership with BOA leadership and, where applicable, program partners.

### 3.2 Recommendation Letter Policy

> **BOA Recommendation Letter Policy**
>
> BOA is proud to offer professional recommendation letters to eligible volunteers. The following guidelines govern the program:
>
> **Eligibility.** A volunteer is eligible for a recommendation letter when they meet at least one of the following criteria:
> - 100 or more hours of verified volunteer service
> - 6 or more months of active participation
> - 3 or more months in a leadership or specialized role
> - Nomination by a BOA officer or team lead
>
> **Authorship.** Recommendation letters are authored by BOA officers, team leads, or designated Senior Volunteers who have directly observed the volunteer's contributions. Authors are encouraged to reference the volunteer's Service Record for specific data.
>
> **Content.** Each letter is personalized, professionally framed, and includes specific examples of the volunteer's contributions, skills, and character. Letters follow the standard BOA Recommendation Letter Template and are formatted on BOA letterhead.
>
> **Delivery.** Letters are delivered as PDF documents within 10 business days of a valid request. Volunteers may request one revision within 14 days of delivery.
>
> **Privacy.** Letters are confidential between the author, the program coordinator, and the requesting volunteer. BOA does not share letters with third parties without the volunteer's explicit consent.
>
> **Non-discrimination.** Recommendation letters are available to all eligible volunteers regardless of role, team, tenure, or personal characteristics. Eligibility criteria are applied uniformly.

### 3.3 Data & Privacy Policy (Recognition Program Addendum)

> **Recognition Program Data & Privacy Addendum**
>
> The Recognition Program collects and maintains volunteer data necessary for service tracking, milestone recognition, and recommendation letter generation. This data includes: volunteer name/identifier, service hours, roles held, participation records, milestone history, and recognition history.
>
> All Recognition Program data is:
> - Collected with the volunteer's informed consent
> - Stored securely in accordance with BOA's data security policies
> - Accessible to the volunteer at any time via self-service tools
> - Private by default — not shared publicly without the volunteer's explicit opt-in
> - Retained for the duration of the volunteer's active service plus 12 months, unless the volunteer requests earlier deletion
> - Available for export by the volunteer at any time
>
> Volunteers may request correction, deletion, or restriction of their Recognition Program data by contacting the program coordinator.

### 3.4 Branding & Co-Branding Policy

> **Recognition Program Branding Policy**
>
> All Recognition Program materials — including certificates, badges, recommendation letters, and public announcements — shall bear BOA branding. Where the program is supported by a partner organization, co-branding may be applied with the approval of BOA leadership and in accordance with the partnership agreement.
>
> Co-branding elements must:
> - Be clearly secondary to BOA branding
> - Not obscure or diminish the volunteer's recognition
> - Be approved in advance by BOA leadership
> - Comply with both BOA and partner brand guidelines

## 5. Implementation Roadmap

The Recognition Program is implemented in four phases, designed to deliver value quickly while building toward the full program vision.

### Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1–4) — *"Launch the Basics"*

**Goal:** Establish the program's infrastructure and begin recognizing volunteers immediately.

| Task | Owner | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Finalize program policy language (Section 3) | BOA Leadership | Approved policy document |
| Design digital badge set (6 tiers + award categories) | Design volunteer / partner | Badge asset pack |
| Create certificate templates (digital, PDF) | Design volunteer / partner | Certificate template files |
| Set up Service Record tracking in BOA Bot (or spreadsheet interim) | BOA Bot team | Functional tracking system |
| Draft and approve recommendation letter template | Program Coordinator | `example-letter-template.md` |
| Announce program to all active volunteers | BOA Leadership | Announcement post + FAQ |
| Train officers and team leads on program processes | Program Coordinator | Training session + guide |

**Quick Win:** Issue "Founding Member" badges to all active volunteers at launch — a one-time recognition that rewards early adopters and generates excitement.

### Phase 2: Activation (Weeks 5–8) — *"Celebrate and Document"*

**Goal:** Begin active milestone tracking, issue first recognitions, and process first recommendation letter requests.

| Task | Owner | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Run first automated milestone detection pass | BOA Bot | Milestone notifications for qualifying volunteers |
| Host first Monthly Community Spotlight | Program Coordinator | Spotlight post / feature |
| Process first batch of recommendation letter requests | Letter authors | Delivered letters |
| Collect volunteer feedback on program (survey) | Program Coordinator | Feedback report |
| Refine badge/certificate designs based on feedback | Design volunteer | Updated assets |
| Publish Skills Translation Guide | Program Coordinator | Guide document |

**Quick Win:** Host a "Recognition Week" — a dedicated week of volunteer spotlights, milestone celebrations, and community gratitude to build program visibility and culture.

### Phase 3: Expansion (Weeks 9–16) — *"Deepen and Systematize"*

**Goal:** Launch Impact Awards, integrate with BOA Bot analytics, and formalize the recommendation letter process.

| Task | Owner | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Launch first Quarterly Impact Awards cycle | Program Coordinator | Awards ceremony + announcements |
| Integrate Service Record export (PDF) into BOA Bot | BOA Bot team | `/export-record` command |
| Build Hall of Service page (web or Discord) | BOA Bot team / web volunteer | Public recognition page |
| Establish recommendation letter author pool and training | Program Coordinator | Trained author roster |
| Develop LinkedIn / résumé guidance resources | Program Coordinator | Resource document |
| Conduct first program review with BOA leadership | Program Coordinator + Leadership | Review report + adjustments |

**Quick Win:** Publish the first annual (or semi-annual) Volunteer Impact Report — a data-rich summary of volunteer contributions, milestones reached, and community impact.

### Phase 4: Maturity (Weeks 17–24) — *"Sustain and Scale"*

**Goal:** Transition to steady-state operations, explore co-branding, and plan for long-term sustainability.

| Task | Owner | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluate program impact (retention data, satisfaction survey) | Program Coordinator | Impact assessment report |
| Finalize co-branding implementation (if applicable) | BOA Leadership + Partner | Co-branded materials |
| Develop succession plan for program coordination | BOA Leadership | Documented handover plan |
| Explore integration with external platforms (LinkedIn badges, Credly, etc.) | BOA Bot team | Integration assessment |
| Plan for Year 2 enhancements based on data and feedback | Program Coordinator + Leadership | Year 2 roadmap |

**Quick Win:** Recognize the program's own contributors — the volunteers and leaders who built and sustained the Recognition Program deserve acknowledgment too.
