## About This Template

This document provides a complete, ready-to-use example of a BOA Volunteer Recommendation Letter. It demonstrates the structure, tone, and level of detail expected for letters issued under the Recognition Program.

Letter authors should use this template as a guide, not a form to fill in mechanically. Every letter should be **personalized** with specific details drawn from the volunteer's Service Record and the author's direct observations. The bracketed sections `[like this]` indicate where authors should insert personalized content.

## Sample Letter

---

**BROTHERHOOD OF ASCENSION**
Ghost of Tabor Community — Volunteer Division

[BOA Logo] · [Partner Logo, if applicable]

---

Date: [Month Day, Year]

To Whom It May Concern,

It is my genuine pleasure to recommend **[Volunteer Name]** for [the position of / admission to / consideration for] **[opportunity — e.g., "the Community Manager role at your organization" / "your graduate program in Information Science" / "any professional opportunity that values dedication, skill, and character"]**.

I have had the privilege of working alongside [Volunteer Name] for **[duration — e.g., "seven months"]** in my capacity as **[Author's Role — e.g., "Onboarding Team Lead for the Brotherhood of Ascension (BOA)"]**, a volunteer-driven community organization serving players of Ghost of Tabor. During this time, [Volunteer Name] has served in the roles of **[Role 1]**, **[Role 2]**, and **[Role 3]**, contributing approximately **[total hours]** hours of verified service.

**[Paragraph 2 — Specific Contributions]**

[Volunteer Name]'s contributions to BOA have been both substantial and distinctive. In their role as **[primary role]**, they [describe a key project or responsibility — e.g., "took ownership of our new player onboarding documentation, authoring a comprehensive 12-part guide series that reduced average new-player time-to-competency by an estimated 30%"]. [He/She/They] also [describe a second key contribution — e.g., "mentored four new volunteers through their first 90 days, providing patient, structured guidance that three of them have cited as the primary reason they remained active in the community"]. When [describe a moment of initiative or going above and beyond — e.g., "our wiki platform experienced a critical data loss incident, [Volunteer Name] voluntarily spent an entire weekend reconstructing the most heavily referenced pages from memory and archived sources, ensuring that the community's knowledge base was restored within 48 hours"].

**[Paragraph 3 — Skills & Competencies]**

Beyond specific projects, [Volunteer Name] has consistently demonstrated skills that I believe translate directly to professional excellence:

- **Communication:** [He/She/They] writes with clarity, warmth, and precision — whether drafting documentation, mediating a disagreement between community members, or presenting a proposal to leadership. [His/Her/Their] written work requires minimal revision and consistently serves its intended audience effectively.

- **Ownership & Initiative:** [Volunteer Name] does not wait to be told what needs doing. [He/She/They] identifies gaps, proposes solutions, and follows through. [He/She/They] treats every task — no matter how small — as something worth doing well.

- **Collaboration:** [He/She/They] works generously with others, shares credit freely, and elevates the people around [him/her/them]. [He/She/They] is the kind of teammate who makes a group greater than the sum of its parts.

- **Adaptability:** [Describe an example — e.g., "When our community shifted to a new communication platform mid-year, [Volunteer Name] not only adapted quickly but created a transition guide that helped dozens of other members navigate the change."]

**[Paragraph 4 — Character]**

I want to speak briefly to [Volunteer Name]'s character, because it is inseparable from [his/her/their] effectiveness. [He/She/They] is [describe character qualities with specific examples — e.g., "genuinely kind — the sort of person who remembers that a fellow volunteer mentioned a difficult week and checks in on them days later. [He/She/They] is also remarkably resilient; when a project [he/she/they] had invested significant effort into was deprioritized due to shifting organizational needs, [he/she/they] adapted without bitterness and immediately redirected [his/her/their] energy to the next priority."] These qualities are not incidental to [his/her/their] contributions — they are the foundation of them.

**[Closing Paragraph]**

I recommend [Volunteer Name] without reservation. [He/She/They] is one of the most [reliable / skilled / dedicated / impactful] volunteers I have worked with during my [duration] of service with BOA, and I am confident that [his/her/their] skills, work ethic, and character will be an asset to any organization or program [he/she/they] joins.

I am happy to provide further context or answer specific questions. I can be reached at **[author's contact — e.g., Discord: @CaptainRook / email: captainrook@boaguild.org]** (with the understanding that I may be in a different time zone and response times may vary).

With sincere respect and confidence,

**[Author Name]**
[Author Title — e.g., Onboarding Team Lead, Brotherhood of Ascension]
[Author Tenure — e.g., BOA Officer since March 2024]

[BOA Digital Seal / Signature]

## Template Usage Guidelines

### For Letter Authors

1. **Read the volunteer's Service Record** before writing. Use specific numbers: hours, roles, milestones, events. Vague praise is less persuasive than concrete detail.

2. **Draw from direct observation.** Write about what you have personally seen or worked on together. If you're co-authoring with someone who has different direct experience, coordinate to cover different aspects.

3. **Be specific, not superlative.** Instead of "Mira is the best writer we've ever had," write "Mira's onboarding guides reduced new-player support questions by an estimated 40% — a measurable impact that speaks to the clarity and thoroughness of their writing."

4. **Match the letter to the purpose.** If the volunteer is applying for a community management role, emphasize communication, conflict resolution, and stakeholder skills. If they're applying for a technical writing role, emphasize documentation quality, information architecture, and audience awareness.

5. **Keep the tone warm but professional.** This is a recommendation, not a performance review. Lead with genuine enthusiasm. The reader should feel that the author truly knows and values this person.

6. **One page is the target.** 300–500 words. If you're running long, prioritize specific examples over general praise.

7. **Include your contact information.** A recommendation letter that can't be verified is less useful. Provide at least one way to reach you.

8. **Proofread.** Then proofread again. This letter represents both the volunteer and BOA. It should be polished.

### For Program Coordinators

1. **Review every letter before delivery.** Check for: personalization (not a form letter), professional tone, correct branding, accurate data, and appropriate length.

2. **Track letter delivery** in the volunteer's Service Record.

3. **Follow up with the volunteer** 2–4 weeks after delivery to ask if the letter was useful and if they need any adjustments or additional letters.

4. **Collect feedback from authors** after each letter is delivered to identify process improvements.

## Customization Notes

### Tone Variations by Purpose

| Purpose | Emphasis | Tone Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Job application | Skills, reliability, teamwork, results | Professional, evidence-focused |
| Academic admission | Intellectual curiosity, growth, character | Reflective, developmental |
| General / open | Well-rounded contribution, character, versatility | Warm, comprehensive |
| Leadership role | Decision-making, mentorship, vision | Authoritative, forward-looking |
| Technical role | Problem-solving, technical skill, precision | Detail-oriented, analytical |

### Co-Branding Note

When the Recognition Program includes partner co-branding, the following additions are made to the letter:

- Partner logo appears below the BOA logo on the letterhead
- A single line is added at the bottom of the letter: *"The BOA Volunteer Recognition Program is supported by [Partner Name]."*
- The letter's content and authorship remain entirely BOA-driven; the partner's role is acknowledged as program support, not as a co-author or evaluator
