## Slide 1: Current State of BOA

**Slide Title:** Where BOA Stands Today

**Key Talking Points:**
- BOA has grown into a vital community pillar, supporting operations across multiple functional areas
- Current membership spans diverse skill sets — from technical contributors to community organizers
- Infrastructure and tooling have not kept pace with organizational growth
- Reliance on informal processes creates inconsistency in how tasks are executed
- Leadership is committed to modernization but requires strategic partnership to scale effectively

**Speaker Notes:**
Good morning, and thank you for joining us. Today we want to share an honest and transparent picture of where BOA currently stands. Over the past several years, BOA has evolved from a small working group into a critical operational backbone for our community. Our volunteers contribute across moderation, event coordination, technical support, and outreach. However, as we've grown, we've encountered growing pains. Our tooling is largely manual, our onboarding process is inconsistent, and institutional knowledge lives in the heads of a few long-standing members rather than in accessible systems. This isn't a criticism of anyone's work — it's a natural consequence of organic growth. The exciting part is that we now have an opportunity to address these gaps together, and that's what this partnership proposal is about.

**Supporting Statistics / Examples:**
- BOA has grown approximately 3× in active membership over the past two years
- Current volunteer base handles an estimated 200+ task requests per month
- Onboarding a new volunteer currently takes 4–6 weeks due to lack of structured training materials
- Approximately 60% of operational knowledge is held by just 20% of the team (concentration risk)

## Slide 2: Volunteer Retention Trends

**Slide Title:** Understanding Our Volunteer Lifecycle

**Key Talking Points:**
- Volunteer turnover has increased over the past 12 months, particularly among members with 3–12 months of tenure
- Exit feedback consistently cites lack of recognition, unclear growth paths, and burnout from repetitive tasks
- New volunteer enthusiasm is high in the first 60 days but drops sharply without structured engagement
- Retained volunteers (12+ months) report strong community bonds but express frustration with process inefficiencies
- Retention is not just a morale issue — it directly impacts operational continuity and institutional knowledge

**Speaker Notes:**
Let's talk about the people who make BOA possible. Our volunteer data tells a story that many growing organizations will recognize. We do an excellent job of attracting passionate people. The challenge is keeping them engaged beyond the initial excitement phase. When we analyzed exit surveys and retention patterns, three themes emerged repeatedly: volunteers want to feel valued, they want to see a path for personal growth, and they're tired of doing the same manual tasks that could be automated. The 3-to-12-month window is our critical danger zone — that's when volunteers either find their long-term niche or quietly disengage. We believe the proposals in this presentation directly address each of these retention drivers, and we'd love to partner with you to make that happen.

**Supporting Statistics / Examples:**
- Overall volunteer retention rate at 12 months: approximately 35% (down from 48% two years ago)
- Volunteers who receive formal recognition are 2.5× more likely to remain active at the 12-month mark
- Average time-to-contribution for new volunteers: 14 days (could be reduced to 3–5 days with better onboarding)
- Top 3 exit reasons cited: burnout (41%), lack of recognition (33%), unclear role expectations (26%)

## Slide 3: Communication Challenges

**Slide Title:** Bridging the Communication Gap

**Key Talking Points:**
- Information is fragmented across multiple platforms (chat, email, spreadsheets, personal notes)
- Critical updates often fail to reach all relevant team members in a time-sensitive manner
- No centralized system for tracking decisions, action items, or project status
- New volunteers struggle to find historical context for ongoing initiatives
- Cross-team collaboration is hindered by siloed communication channels

**Speaker Notes:**
Communication is the connective tissue of any organization, and right now, ours is stretched thin. We currently rely on a patchwork of chat channels, email threads, and shared documents to coordinate our work. The result is that important information falls through the cracks. A volunteer might miss a critical policy change because it was buried in a chat channel they don't monitor. A project update might be known to one sub-team but completely invisible to another. For new team members, the situation is even more challenging — there's no single source of truth they can consult to get up to speed. This isn't just an inconvenience; it leads to duplicated effort, missed deadlines, and frustration. We see a real opportunity here to implement unified communication practices that would benefit not just BOA, but the broader ecosystem we operate within.

**Supporting Statistics / Examples:**
- Volunteers report spending an average of 45 minutes per day searching for information or context
- An estimated 30% of task requests are duplicated because teams aren't aware of parallel efforts
- Only 25% of organizational decisions from the past year are documented in an accessible format
- Communication-related friction is the #1 complaint in quarterly volunteer satisfaction surveys

## Slide 5: Operational Burden and Manual Processes

**Slide Title:** The Hidden Cost of Doing Things Manually

**Key Talking Points:**
- Routine operational tasks (scheduling, reporting, onboarding checklists) consume disproportionate staff time
- Manual data entry and tracking introduce errors that require additional time to correct
- Lack of automation means skilled volunteers spend time on work that doesn't leverage their expertise
- Reporting and metrics compilation is done manually, delaying insights and decision-making
- Scaling operations without process improvement is unsustainable — more work requires more people, not smarter systems

**Speaker Notes:**
This is the slide that keeps our leadership team up at night. Right now, some of our most talented volunteers — people with deep technical skills, creative problem-solving abilities, and strategic thinking — are spending a significant portion of their time on tasks that could be automated or streamlined. Think about onboarding: every time we bring in a new volunteer, a senior member has to manually walk them through processes that haven't changed in months. Reporting is another example — compiling our monthly activity report takes roughly 20 person-hours of manual data gathering and formatting. That's 20 hours that could have been spent on high-impact project work. The math is simple: if we want to grow our impact without burning out our team, we need to reduce the operational burden. This is one of the areas where we believe a partnership could deliver the most immediate and measurable return.

**Supporting Statistics / Examples:**
- Estimated 35% of total volunteer hours are spent on repetitive, automatable tasks
- Monthly reporting process: ~20 person-hours of manual compilation
- Onboarding checklist: 47 individual steps, 80% of which are identical for every new volunteer
- Error rate in manually tracked data: approximately 8–12%, requiring rework
- If automation freed up even 50% of repetitive-task hours, BOA could take on an estimated 40% more project work

## Slide 6: BOA Bot Proposal

**Slide Title:** Introducing the BOA Bot — Our Automation Opportunity

**Key Talking Points:**
- A purpose-built bot to handle routine communications, task routing, and status updates
- Automated onboarding workflows: welcome sequences, resource delivery, progress tracking
- Real-time notifications for task assignments, deadlines, and priority changes
- Integration with existing platforms to reduce context-switching for volunteers
- Built-in analytics to surface operational insights without manual reporting
- Designed with volunteer experience top-of-mind: reduce friction, not add complexity

**Speaker Notes:**
This is where we get to the exciting part — our first major proposal. We envision a BOA Bot: a lightweight, purpose-built automation tool that handles the routine operational tasks currently consuming our volunteers' time. Think of it as a digital operations assistant. When a new volunteer joins, the bot manages their onboarding journey — delivering resources, tracking progress, and alerting mentors at key milestones. When a task comes in, the bot routes it to the right team based on skills and availability. When a deadline approaches, it sends proactive reminders. And behind the scenes, it generates the metrics and reports that currently take 20 hours to compile manually. We're not talking about replacing human judgment — we're talking about eliminating the busywork that gets in the way of human judgment. We believe this is a high-impact, achievable project that would demonstrate the value of this partnership from day one.

**Supporting Statistics / Examples:**
- Similar automation bots in comparable communities have reduced onboarding time by 60–70%
- Task routing automation can cut average assignment time from 4 hours to under 15 minutes
- Automated reporting could save an estimated 240 person-hours per year
- Pilot estimate: a functional MVP could be developed in 8–12 weeks with dedicated technical support
- Expected ROI: for every hour invested in bot development, approximately 15 hours of volunteer time saved annually

## Slide 8: Knowledge Base Initiative

**Slide Title:** Preserving and Sharing Our Collective Knowledge

**Key Talking Points:**
- Centralized, searchable knowledge base to capture institutional knowledge and best practices
- Structured documentation for all recurring processes, policies, and procedures
- Community-contributed articles, tutorials, and case studies
- Reduces onboarding time and eliminates single points of failure for critical knowledge
- Living resource that grows with the organization — updated continuously, not a one-time project
- Open-access model encourages knowledge sharing across the broader community ecosystem

**Speaker Notes:**
One of BOA's greatest assets is the depth of knowledge our team has accumulated over the years. Our greatest vulnerability is that most of that knowledge exists in people's heads, in old chat threads, and in scattered documents. If our most experienced volunteers were to step away tomorrow, we'd lose irreplaceable institutional memory. That's why we're proposing a Knowledge Base Initiative — a centralized, living repository where we capture, organize, and share everything our team knows. This includes process documentation, decision rationale, troubleshooting guides, lessons learned from past projects, and best practices. The beauty of this initiative is that it's not just an internal tool. By making portions of the knowledge base accessible to the broader community, we position BOA as a thought leader and resource hub. It also dramatically reduces onboarding time — instead of relying on one-on-one knowledge transfer, new volunteers can self-serve the information they need, when they need it.

**Supporting Statistics / Examples:**
- Communities with documented knowledge bases report 50–60% faster onboarding for new contributors
- Single-point-of-failure risk: currently, 5 volunteers hold exclusive knowledge of critical processes
- Searchable documentation reduces "how do I..." questions by an estimated 40–50%
- Knowledge base content can be repurposed for blog posts, tutorials, and external communications
- Comparable organizations report that every $1 invested in knowledge management yields $3–5 in productivity gains
