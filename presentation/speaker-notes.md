## Slide 1: Where BOA Stands Today

Good morning, and thank you all for being here today. I want to start by painting an honest picture of where BOA currently stands — not to dwell on shortcomings, but to establish a shared understanding of our starting point.

Over the past several years, BOA has grown from a small, informal working group into something much more significant. We now support operations across moderation, event coordination, technical development, community outreach, and incident response. Our volunteers bring an incredible range of skills and dedication. That's the good news.

The challenge is that our growth has been organic — and while that organic quality gives us a strong community culture, it also means our infrastructure hasn't kept pace. We rely heavily on informal processes, personal relationships, and institutional memory held by a small number of long-standing members. Our onboarding is inconsistent. Our documentation is sparse. Our tools are manual.

None of this is anyone's fault. It's the natural result of a passionate group of people doing their best with limited resources. But it does mean that if we want to scale — if we want to increase our impact without burning out our team — we need to make some strategic investments. And that's exactly what this partnership proposal is about.

I want to emphasize: this isn't about fixing something broken. It's about investing in something that already works and helping it reach its full potential. We're excited to explore how we can do that together.

## Slide 2: Understanding Our Volunteer Lifecycle

Now let's talk about the people who make BOA possible — our volunteers. Understanding our retention trends is critical because everything we do depends on their sustained engagement.

The data tells a clear story. We're very good at attracting passionate, skilled people. Our recruitment pipeline is strong, and new volunteers typically arrive with tremendous energy and enthusiasm. The challenge is what happens next.

Our retention data shows a significant drop-off in the 3-to-12-month window. Volunteers in this range are at the highest risk of disengagement. When we look at exit surveys and feedback from long-standing members, three themes come up again and again.

First, volunteers want to feel valued. They want their contributions to be seen and appreciated — not just by the people they work with directly, but by the organization as a whole.

Second, they want a growth path. Volunteers who can see how their role might evolve, how they might take on new challenges or develop new skills, are far more likely to stay engaged.

Third — and this is a big one — they're frustrated by repetitive manual work. Many of our volunteers have significant technical and professional skills, and they joined BOA to apply those skills to meaningful problems. When they spend their time on tasks that feel like busywork, motivation erodes quickly.

The good news is that each of these drivers is addressable. And the proposals in this presentation are designed to tackle all three head-on.

## Slide 3: Bridging the Communication Gap

Let's shift to communication — the connective tissue of any organization. And right now, I'll be honest: ours needs attention.

We currently coordinate our work across a patchwork of chat channels, email threads, shared spreadsheets, and personal notes. There's no single source of truth. There's no centralized system for tracking decisions, action items, or project status.

What does this look like in practice? It looks like a volunteer missing a critical policy update because it was posted in a channel they don't regularly monitor. It looks like two teams unknowingly working on the same task because there's no shared visibility into who's doing what. It looks like a new team member spending their first week just trying to figure out where information lives.

We've estimated that our volunteers spend an average of 45 minutes per day just searching for information or context. That's nearly four hours per week per volunteer that could be spent on actual productive work. Across our entire team, that's a staggering amount of lost productivity.

Beyond the efficiency cost, there's a cultural cost. When communication is fragmented, people feel out of the loop. They feel like they're working in isolation rather than as part of a team. That erodes the sense of community that is BOA's greatest strength.

We see a real opportunity to implement unified communication practices — and this is an area where partnership support could make an immediate, tangible difference.

## Slide 4: Celebrating What We've Built Together

Before we go further into proposals and plans, I want to take a moment to celebrate what BOA has accomplished. Because the track record is genuinely impressive, and it's important context for everything that follows.

Since our founding, BOA has been behind some of the most impactful initiatives this community has seen. Our event team has coordinated over 150 community gatherings — workshops, meetups, hackathons, and more — that have brought together thousands of participants. Our technical contributors have built and maintained a suite of tools that the entire community relies on daily.

But I'm most proud of our mentorship culture. Over 50 community members who started as BOA volunteers have gone on to take on leadership roles, lead their own projects, and mentor the next generation of contributors. That's a legacy that compounds over time.

And when things have gone wrong — when incidents have threatened our community's stability — it's been BOA members who stepped up. Often working around the clock, often without recognition, they've protected the integrity of our community time and time again.

So when we talk about investing in BOA, we're not talking about rescuing a struggling organization. We're talking about investing in a proven, high-impact team and giving them the resources to do even more of what they do best.

## Slide 5: The Hidden Cost of Doing Things Manually

Now I want to talk about something that doesn't show up in our success stories but has a profound impact on our day-to-day operations: the burden of manual processes.

Right now, a significant portion of our volunteer hours — we estimate about 35% — is spent on tasks that are repetitive, routine, and highly amenable to automation. I'm talking about things like onboarding checklists, scheduling coordination, data entry, status reporting, and task tracking.

Let me give you a concrete example. Every month, compiling our activity report takes approximately 20 person-hours. That's 20 hours of someone gathering data from multiple sources, formatting it, reconciling discrepancies, and preparing it for review. That process could be largely automated.

Onboarding is another example. We have a 47-step onboarding checklist. Eighty percent of those steps are identical for every new volunteer. Yet each time, a senior team member has to manually walk through the process, track follow-ups, and ensure nothing falls through the cracks.

The opportunity cost is enormous. Every hour a skilled volunteer spends on manual busywork is an hour they're not spending on the high-impact, meaningful work they joined BOA to do. And as our operational demands grow, the only way to keep up without process improvement is to keep adding more people — which isn't sustainable.

If we could automate even half of our repetitive tasks, we estimate BOA could take on 40% more project work without adding a single new volunteer. That's the power of working smarter.

## Slide 6: Introducing the BOA Bot — Our Automation Opportunity

This brings us to our first major proposal: the BOA Bot.

We envision a lightweight, purpose-built automation tool — a digital operations assistant — that handles the routine tasks currently consuming our volunteers' time and attention.

Here's what the BOA Bot would do. When a new volunteer joins, the bot manages their onboarding journey: delivering resources at the right pace, tracking completion, alerting mentors when check-ins are due, and ensuring no step is missed. When a task request comes in, the bot routes it to the appropriate team based on skills, availability, and current workload. When deadlines approach, it sends proactive reminders to the right people.

Behind the scenes, the bot generates real-time metrics and reports. The 20-hour monthly report? Automated. The weekly status updates? Automated. The task completion tracking? Automated.

I want to be clear about what this is not. This is not about replacing human judgment or removing the personal touch that makes BOA special. It's about eliminating the friction that gets in the way of human judgment. It's about giving our volunteers the tools to focus on what they do best.

We believe this is a high-impact, achievable project. Our initial estimate is that a functional MVP could be developed in 8 to 12 weeks with dedicated technical support. And the return on investment is compelling: for every hour invested in developing the bot, we estimate approximately 15 hours of volunteer time saved annually.

This is the kind of initiative that could demonstrate the value of this partnership from day one.

## Slide 7: Recognizing the People Behind the Impact

Our second proposal is close to our hearts: a formal volunteer Recognition Program.

Let me start with a simple truth: BOA's volunteers are extraordinary. They donate their time, their expertise, and their energy to make this community better. And right now, our recognition of that contribution is inconsistent at best.

Most of our appreciation happens informally — a thank-you in a chat channel, a shout-out during a meeting. Those moments matter, but they're not enough. They don't scale, they're not visible to the broader community, and they don't create a lasting record of contribution.

We're proposing a structured recognition program with several components. First, a tiered milestone system: digital badges and certificates that volunteers earn as they reach contribution milestones — events coordinated, tasks completed, mentees guided, years of service. Second, quarterly Impact Awards, peer-nominated, that highlight exceptional contributions across different categories. Third, featured spotlights — stories that go beyond the numbers to capture the human impact of volunteer work.

We'd also love to explore co-branding opportunities here. When our partner's support of volunteer recognition is visible — on award certificates, in spotlight features, during celebration events — it strengthens mutual visibility and reinforces the partnership's value to the community.

The research on recognition is unambiguous: organizations with formal recognition programs see 30 to 40 percent higher volunteer retention. In our own surveys, 78% of BOA volunteers said that more recognition would significantly increase their satisfaction. This is a high-impact, low-cost initiative that we could launch quickly and iterate over time.

## Slide 8: Preserving and Sharing Our Collective Knowledge

Our third proposal addresses one of our most significant vulnerabilities: the concentration of institutional knowledge.

Right now, the vast majority of BOA's operational knowledge — our processes, our decision rationale, our troubleshooting expertise, our lessons learned — lives in the heads of a small number of long-standing members. If those individuals were to step away, we'd lose irreplaceable knowledge that took years to accumulate.

This is not a hypothetical risk. We've already experienced situations where a departing volunteer took critical process knowledge with them, and the team had to rebuild that understanding from scratch.

Our proposal is a Knowledge Base Initiative: a centralized, searchable, living repository where we capture and organize everything our team knows. This includes standardized process documentation, decision logs, troubleshooting guides, project retrospectives, and best practice articles.

The benefits are immediate and compounding. New volunteers can self-serve the information they need instead of relying on one-on-one knowledge transfer. Existing team members spend less time answering repetitive questions. Critical knowledge is preserved regardless of individual turnover.

But this isn't just an internal tool. By making portions of the knowledge base accessible to the broader community, we position BOA as a thought leader and resource hub. Content from the knowledge base can be repurposed into blog posts, tutorials, and conference talks — extending our reach and impact.

We see this as a foundational investment that makes every other initiative more effective. When your processes are documented, automation is easier. When your knowledge is shared, onboarding is faster. When your expertise is visible, partnerships are stronger.

## Slide 9: Expanding Our Technical Footprint Through Partnership

I want to spend a few minutes on an opportunity that I think is particularly exciting: technical knowledge sharing.

BOA isn't just an operations team. We're a community of technical practitioners who have developed real, hands-on expertise through the work we do. Our contributors have built and maintained infrastructure, developed community tools, implemented security practices, and solved data challenges — all at community scale.

We see a tremendous opportunity to share this expertise through joint technical programming with our partner. Let me paint a picture of what this could look like.

Imagine a workshop series where BOA technical leads share lessons learned from building and maintaining community-scale tools — the challenges, the trade-offs, the solutions that emerged from real-world constraints. Simultaneously, your team shares enterprise-grade best practices, giving our contributors exposure to professional methodologies and tools they might not otherwise encounter.

Imagine collaborative open-source projects that serve both our community's needs and your technical objectives. Our contributors get mentorship and exposure to professional development practices. Your team gets fresh perspectives from practitioners solving real problems at scale.

Imagine co-authored technical content — blog posts, case studies, conference talks — that amplifies visibility for both organizations and positions us as thought leaders in the space.

This kind of technical exchange is genuinely mutual. It's not charity in either direction. Both sides bring valuable expertise to the table, and both sides benefit from the exchange. And the broader community benefits from the shared output.
